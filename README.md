# Crisis-Recovery-Analysis

## QuickBite Express Crisis Recovery Analysis

## Executive Summary

QuickBite Express is a Bengaluru-based food-tech startup operating in the food delivery and consumer analytics space. In June 2025, the company faced a major crisis triggered by a viral food safety incident involving partner restaurants, along with a delivery outage during the monsoon season. The combined impact led to a sharp fall in customer trust, weaker delivery performance, and significant business disruption.

This analysis compares pre-crisis performance from January to May 2025 with crisis-period performance from June to September 2025 to identify what changed, where the business was hit hardest, and what recovery actions should be prioritized.

The analysis found that the crisis was not a normal seasonal dip. It was a structural business shock. Average monthly orders fell by **61.2%**, estimated revenue loss reached **₹25.78 million**, average delivery time increased from **39 minutes to 60 minutes**, cancellation rate rose from **6.1% to 11.9%**, and average customer rating declined from **4.50 to 2.51**.

The findings suggest that QuickBite’s biggest challenge is not only customer acquisition, but **trust recovery**. The most effective recovery path is to fix operational reliability, rebuild quality perception, retain high-value customers, and protect valuable restaurant partnerships.

---

## Business Problem

QuickBite management needed a data-driven crisis recovery strategy after a major operational and reputational breakdown in June 2025.

The business needed answers to the following questions:

- Which customer segments can still be recovered, and which require a different strategy?
- How did customer order behavior change across pre-crisis, crisis, and recovery phases?
- How badly were delivery performance, cancellations, and service-level reliability affected?
- Which campaigns can rebuild trust and loyalty across different customer groups?
- Which restaurant partnerships are most valuable for long-term retention?
- How can customer feedback, ratings, and sentiment be monitored to support ongoing recovery?

This project was built to convert raw operational and customer data into actionable business insights that support decision-making in crisis recovery, customer retention, and platform trust rebuilding.

## Methodology

### 1. Business Understanding
The project began by understanding the business context behind the QuickBite crisis. The goal was not only to measure the decline, but also to identify the drivers of trust erosion and recovery opportunities.

### 2. Data Cleaning and Preparation
The dataset was cleaned and prepared using **SQL Server Management Studio** and **Excel**. SQL was used for structured querying, filtering, and analysis, while Excel was used for smaller tasks and functions that were quicker to perform outside SQL.

### 3. Phase-Based Analysis
The analysis was divided into two key periods:

- **Pre-crisis:** January to May 2025
- **Crisis period:** June to September 2025

This comparison helped identify whether the business decline was temporary or structurally linked to the June 2025 incident.

### 4. KPI and Performance Analysis
Key metrics were analysed across both periods, including:

- Orders
- Revenue impact
- Delivery time
- Cancellation rate
- Customer ratings
- City-level performance
- Customer-level behavior
- Restaurant-level stress indicators
- Review sentiment themes

### 5. Dashboard Development
Power BI was used to convert the analytical outputs into a visual dashboard. DAX measures were created to support KPI tracking, comparison logic, and business storytelling.

### 6. Insight Generation and Strategy Framing
The final step focused on turning findings into practical recovery recommendations around trust rebuilding, operational improvement, customer win-back, and restaurant retention.

## Results

### Overall Business Impact
- Average monthly orders dropped from **22,761** in the pre-crisis period to **8,840** during the crisis period
- This represented a **61.2% decline** in demand
- Estimated revenue loss was **₹25.78 million**
- The data shows a clear structural break in June, and demand did not recover in the following months

### Customer Experience Deterioration
- Average delivery time increased from **39 minutes** to **60 minutes**
- Cancellation rate increased from **6.1%** to **11.9%**
- Average customer rating fell from **4.50** to **2.51**
- These indicators show that speed, reliability, and customer satisfaction all deteriorated at the same time

### Sentiment and Trust Breakdown
Customer reviews showed that the crisis was not just about delivery delays. The strongest complaint themes were:

- Food quality is not good
- Food safety issue
- Cold food
- Portion size smaller than expected
- Not worth the price

This indicates that the crisis created a combined failure across service quality, food quality perception, and value perception.

### Geographic Impact
The highest absolute order decline was seen in:

- Bengaluru
- Mumbai
- Delhi
- Hyderabad
- Chennai

These cities showed the deepest demand loss and should be treated as priority recovery markets.

### Customer and Partner Risk
- High-value customers reduced order frequency and also showed major rating drops
- This suggests loss of repeat revenue and lower lifetime value
- Several high-volume restaurants showed steep performance decline, signaling merchant-level stress
- Smaller independent restaurants and dine-in partners appear more vulnerable, while cloud kitchens appear more resilient

Overall, the crisis damaged both sides of the platform:

- **Demand side:** customer trust, satisfaction, and retention
- **Supply side:** restaurant confidence and platform dependency

## Recommendations

### 1. Fix Reliability First
- Reduce late deliveries in the worst-affected cities
- Build city-level crisis monitoring for Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Delhi
- Track cancellations and SLA performance daily
- Restore service consistency before scaling campaigns

### 2. Rebuild Trust Visibly
- Launch stronger food safety audits for affected restaurant clusters
- Communicate food safety standards, packaging quality, and handling improvements to customers
- Make trust recovery visible rather than assumed

### 3. Target High-Value Customers First
- Prioritize churn-risk, high-frequency, and high-value users for recovery offers
- Use personalized win-back campaigns instead of broad discounting
- Offer service recovery credits, free delivery, or targeted cashback where appropriate

### 4. Protect Restaurant Partnerships
- Identify restaurant partners with the highest long-term value
- Support vulnerable merchants with visibility boosts and quality improvement measures
- Strengthen partnerships with restaurants that are critical for retention and assortment quality

### 5. Use Incentives Carefully
- Cashback can support recovery, but it should not be the first lever
- Trust and operations have higher recovery impact than cashback alone
- Incentives should be used after service reliability and food quality improvements are in place

## Conclusion

The QuickBite crisis was not just a short-term demand decline. It was a trust breakdown caused by simultaneous failures in delivery reliability, customer experience, and food quality perception.

The business recovery strategy should therefore focus on restoring trust first, stabilizing operations second, and then reactivating demand through targeted customer and partner initiatives.

This project demonstrates how data analytics can be used not just to measure business decline, but to diagnose root causes and guide strategic recovery decisions.
